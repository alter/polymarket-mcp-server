# Predicting Replicability: Surveys and Prediction Markets

## Abstract

The researchers analyzed data from four studies that used prediction markets and surveys to forecast replication outcomes in social and behavioral sciences. They pooled data on 103 published findings to evaluate how well these methods could predict whether research would replicate. Published in PLOS ONE, April 2021.

## Key Findings

1. **Prediction Market Accuracy:** Prediction markets can determine the outcomes of direct replications with 73% accuracy (n=103)

2. **Survey Comparison:** Both prediction market prices and survey responses showed significant correlation with actual replication outcomes (0.581 and 0.564 respectively, p < .001). Prediction surveys were correct 66% of the time (68/103).

3. **Information in Community:** The research demonstrates that there is information within the scientific community about the replicability of scientific findings

4. **P-value Relationship:** A meaningful association exists between the original findings' p-values and their replication success

5. **Dataset Availability:** Made publicly available through an R package called pooledmaRket

Connects to reproducibility crisis literature — costs of irreproducible preclinical research estimated at US$28 billion per year in the United States.
