# Models, Markets, and the Forecasting of Elections

## Abstract

The researchers compared probabilistic election forecasts from two sources during the 2020 US presidential election: The Economist's published model and predictions from the PredictIt prediction market. They analyzed seven months of daily data for battleground states.

## Key Findings

1. **Temporal Performance Differences:** Markets demonstrated superior accuracy several months before election day, while the statistical model performed better as voting approached.

2. **Hybrid Approach Effectiveness:** A simple average of the two forecasts performs better than either one of them overall, despite neither forecast combination outperforming both individual sources simultaneously for any specific state-date combination.

3. **Complementary Error Patterns:** The forecasting methods made different types of mistakes in different locations — the model showed overconfidence in certain states while markets displayed excessive uncertainty in others.

4. **Practical Applications:** The authors recommend hybrid forecasting strategies and propose a market design using algorithmic trading to integrate model predictions with market prices.
