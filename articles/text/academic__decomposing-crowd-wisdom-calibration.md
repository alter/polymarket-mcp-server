# Decomposing Crowd Wisdom: Domain-Specific Calibration Dynamics in Prediction Markets

## Abstract

The paper examines whether prediction market prices accurately reflect true probabilities. Using data from 292 million trades across 327,000 contracts on Kalshi and Polymarket, the researcher identifies systematic patterns in how well prices calibrate to actual outcomes.

## Key Findings

1. **Decomposition Model:** Calibration variance breaks down into four components explaining 87.3% of the total variance: a universal time-horizon effect, domain-specific biases, domain-by-horizon interactions, and a trade-size scale effect.

2. **Political Market Bias:** Prices are chronically compressed toward 50% in political markets across both exchanges, indicating persistent underconfidence in these predictions.

3. **Platform Differences:** Large trades amplify underconfidence in political contracts on Kalshi (Δ = 0.53) but show minimal effect on Polymarket (Δ = 0.11), suggesting distinct market microstructures.

4. **Practical Implication:** Market participants who treat quoted prices as literal probabilities will systematically misinterpret them, with the direction and magnitude of error depending on the market domain, timeframe, and trade characteristics.
