# Favorite-Longshot Bias: An Overview of the Main Explanations

**Source:** ResearchGate survey of the literature
**Related paper:** Risk aversion and favourite–longshot bias in a competitive fixed‐odds betting market (Whelan, Economica 2024)

## Overview of the Bias

**Favorite-longshot bias:** In betting and prediction markets, longshots (low-probability outcomes) are systematically overpriced relative to their true chances, while favorites (high-probability outcomes) are underpriced.

## Empirical Evidence

**Traditional Markets:**
- Horse racing: longshots lose more than favorites (Ali 1977)
- Fixed-odds betting markets: consistent longshot bias in most sports
- Exception: reverse longshot bias in baseball and hockey (overpriced favorites)

**Modern Prediction Platforms (Kalshi, Polymarket):**
- Classic favorite-longshot bias present but horizon-dependent
- Calibration slope decreases from ~1.32 (>1 month horizon) to ~0.99 (<1 hour to resolution)
- Near-perfect calibration and near-complete disappearance of bias close to resolution

**Domain variation:**
- Strongest in markets with many casual participants (sports, entertainment, novelty)
- Weaker in political/financial markets with more sophisticated participants

## Causal Explanations

1. **Risk-seeking preferences:** Bettors prefer longshots for their lottery-like properties
2. **Information asymmetry:** Bookmakers set odds to discourage bets on longshots (risk management)
3. **Disagreement among uninformed bettors:** Causes longshot overpricing
4. **Market thinness:** Thin markets amplify the bias; liquid markets reduce it
5. **Herding behavior:** Confirmation of public opinion pushes favorites even further

## Horizon Effect in Prediction Markets

Calibration slope by time-to-resolution:
- >30 days: slope ≈ 1.32 (strong underconfidence)
- 7–30 days: slope ≈ 1.15
- 1–7 days: slope ≈ 1.05
- <1 hour: slope ≈ 0.99 (near-perfect calibration)

## Relevance to Prediction-Market Pricing

The favorite-longshot bias is a systematic and exploitable pricing inefficiency in Polymarket:
- At long horizons: buy YES on favorites (underpriced), sell YES on longshots (overpriced)
- The bias is strongest in entertainment/sports categories
- The horizon effect means the bias is largest when markets are opened and smallest near resolution
- Strategies should fade longshots early in a market's life, especially in retail-dominated categories
