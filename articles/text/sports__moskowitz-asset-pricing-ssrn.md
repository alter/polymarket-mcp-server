# Asset Pricing and Sports Betting

**Author:** Tobias J. Moskowitz (Yale University, Yale SOM; AQR Capital; NBER)  
**Published:** *The Journal of Finance*, 2021, Vol 76, No 6, pp. 3153–3209  
**SSRN:** abstract_id=2635517  
**PDF (public):** https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/finance/docs/pdfs/Seminars/moskowitz_seminar_paper.pdf

## Core Motivation

Sports betting markets serve as a laboratory to test behavioral theories of cross-sectional asset pricing anomalies. Two unique features provide a distinguishing test:

1. **No systematic risk**: Bets are completely idiosyncratic — no relation to aggregate risk premia
2. **Known termination date**: Contracts have short, known resolution dates where uncertainty is resolved by outcomes independent of betting activity

This evades the joint hypothesis problem, allowing mispricing to be directly detected.

## Data

- **100,000+ contracts** spanning two decades
- Four major US professional sports: NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL
- Betting price data from open to close

## Key Findings

### Momentum Effect
- Strong evidence of **momentum in betting prices** (open-to-close)
- Consistent with **delayed overreaction** — inconsistent with underreaction and rational pricing
- "Bettors follow momentum and value signals that push prices away from fundamentals"

### Value Effect
- Value signals (betting on "cheap" contracts) move prices from open to close
- Both momentum and value signals fully **reverse from close to game outcome** — confirming genuine mispricing rather than rational adjustment

### Magnitude vs. Financial Markets
- Returns per unit of risk are approximately **1/5 as large** as those found in financial markets
- Returns fail to overcome transactions costs — "preventing arbitrage from eliminating them"
- No covariance structure for value or momentum returns across sports betting contracts (unlike financial markets)

## Implications for Financial Markets

The behavioral mechanisms found in sports betting (delayed overreaction, momentum chasing) appear to also predict momentum and value returns in financial markets. Sports betting provides a clean test because idiosyncratic risk rules out rational explanations.

## Transferable Edge

1. **Momentum at contract open is a buy signal** — early movers create overreaction that can be faded near close
2. **Value in betting markets** is real and mirrors financial market value effects
3. Transactions costs must be modeled — sports betting edges are small enough that vig/spread matter enormously
4. The delayed overreaction finding directly applies to Polymarket: late-settling contracts where early money has driven prices away from fundamentals
