# Two Arguments for Fractional Kelly

**Author:** Harry Crane

## Argument 1: Information Limitation and Learning Speed

The first argument centers on the practical reality that bettors never possess complete information about their edge. "Variance prevents us from knowing what our edge actually is — or if we have an edge at all."

When using full Kelly with high variance, significant losses occur before bettors can definitively determine whether their strategy actually works. Fractional Kelly reduces this variance, allowing bettors to gather sufficient evidence more quickly about whether their estimated edge is real. This prevents catastrophic losses while still operating under uncertainty.

## Argument 2: Decision-Making Under Drawdown Conditions

The second argument addresses what happens during inevitable losing streaks. "Variance makes losers think they're winners, and winners think they're losers."

By using fractional Kelly, bettors can establish clearer stopping rules based on likelihood ratios and maximum exposure thresholds. This framework enables more decisive conclusions about when to abandon a strategy.

With full Kelly, even substantial drawdowns offer ambiguous evidence, potentially trapping bettors between continuing a failing strategy or abandoning a profitable one.

## Core Insight

Both arguments emphasize that fractional Kelly's value lies not in reducing variance per se, but in facilitating faster, more confident **learning about edge validity** before resources are exhausted.

## Relevance

A non-standard perspective: fractional Kelly is rational not just because of overbetting risk, but because it allows better hypothesis testing about whether your edge is real.
