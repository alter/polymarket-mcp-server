# What Happens When Institutional Liquidity Enters Prediction Markets

**Author:** Shaw Dalen

**Submitted:** April 11, 2026 (v1); April 17, 2026 (v2)

**arXiv:** 2604.10005

## Abstract

Prediction markets are starting to look less like crowd polls and more like electronic markets. The central question is no longer only whether these markets forecast well, but what happens when institutional liquidity enters: do spreads tighten, does price discovery improve, and do those gains actually reach the traders who are slowest to react when information arrives?

## Key Findings

1. **Market-maker coverage, liquidity incentives, and automation do not work through the same channel** — they have separable effects on spread and price discovery
2. **Average liquidity gains do not translate into equal gains for all traders** — institutional entry benefits fast traders more
3. **Sharpest welfare losses appear in shock states** — when slower takers receive the least pass-through of tighter quoted markets
4. Provides a **synthetic microstructure laboratory** as proof of concept for measurement pipeline
5. Identifies the main channels through which institutional liquidity enters prediction markets

## Relevance to Polymarket CLOB Trading

- Confirms spread tightening from institutional market makers is beneficial for retail takers on average
- Shock-state adverse selection falls disproportionately on slower traders — timing matters
- Provides research design framework for measuring how maker incentives affect market quality
- Relevant for understanding how to position as market maker vs. informed taker on Polymarket
