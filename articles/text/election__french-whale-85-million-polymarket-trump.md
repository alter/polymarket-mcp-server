# How a French Whale Made $85 Million off Trump's Win

**Source:** The Free Press  
**Author:** Joe Nocera

## Overview

An anonymous French trader operating under the pseudonym "Théo" made approximately $85 million betting on Trump's 2024 election victory through Polymarket, a prediction market platform.

## Key Details

**The Trader:** Goes by the pseudonym "Théo," chose not to disclose his real identity. He is a French national with extensive trading experience and a financial services background. His Polymarket usernames included Fredi9999, Theo4, PrincessCaro, and Michie.

**The Strategy:** Théo commissioned a YouGov poll asking respondents to predict how their neighbors would vote, rather than directly asking about personal preferences — a technique known as "neighbor polling." He believed this approach would reveal hidden support for Trump among "shy voters" who typically avoid pollsters.

**The Conviction:** After seeing the poll results in late October, Théo became confident enough to place massive bets. He initially wagered $30 million on Trump, then escalated to $80 million using 11 different cryptocurrency accounts after reviewing the polling data. He sold off virtually all his liquid assets to fund the wager.

**The Insight:** Théo wrote that "most of the polls were skewed to the advantage of Harris by ignoring the shy Trump voter effect." He had "absolutely no political agenda" and made the bets after becoming convinced polling had underestimated the "shy Trump voter effect."

**The Result:** His bet proved correct, yielding approximately $85 million in winnings — a remarkable return that demonstrated his polling methodology successfully predicted an election outcome that professional pollsters had forecasted as a close race.

## Account Rankings

Theo4 and Fredi9999 ranked as the first and second most profitable accounts of all time on Polymarket; PrincessCaro and Michie are in eighth and fourteenth place, respectively.

## Regulatory Fallout

France's Autorité Nationale des Jeux (ANJ), the country's gambling authority, took issue with his trade. Online gambling is tightly regulated in France, and operating any new gambling market is subject to prior authorization from the ANJ. The ANJ expected to ban access to Polymarket for French users.

## Market Impact

The scale of the bets drew intense media scrutiny and sparked fears of market manipulation. The Wall Street Journal reported the market moves might be a mirage created by "four bettors." Blockchain analysis by Chainalysis linked 11 accounts to the same whale trader.

Polymarket conducted an investigation and determined there was no evidence of market manipulation, noting the trader intentionally spread his bets across smaller positions to avoid moving the market erratically.

## Broader Context

Polymarket CEO Shayne Coplan noted the case demonstrates how "markets that are big enough create incentive for people to go above and beyond to try and find truth," suggesting the financial stakes motivated superior research methodology.
