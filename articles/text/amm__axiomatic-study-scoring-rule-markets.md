# An Axiomatic Study of Scoring Rule Markets

**Authors:** Rafael Frongillo, Bo Waggoner

**arXiv ID:** 1709.10065 | Published at ITCS 2018

## Abstract

Prediction markets are well-studied in the case where predictions are probabilities or expectations of future random variables. In 2008, Lambert et al. proposed a generalization, which we call "scoring rule markets" (SRMs), in which traders predict the value of arbitrary statistics of the random variables, provided these statistics can be elicited by a scoring rule. To initiate such a study, we ask: in what sense are SRMs "markets"? We classify SRMs according to several axioms that capture potentially desirable qualities of a market, such as the ability to freely exchange goods (contracts) for money. Not all SRMs satisfy our axioms: once a contract is purchased in any market for prediction the median of some variable, there will not necessarily be any way to sell that contract back. Our main result is a characterization showing that slight generalizations of cost-function-based markets are the only markets to satisfy all of our axioms for finite-outcome random variables.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces foundational axioms for evaluating scoring rule markets as legitimate market mechanisms
- Demonstrates limitations of certain SRM structures (e.g., median prediction markets lack liquidity — the trade neutralization axiom fails)
- Characterizes cost-function-based markets as the unique solution satisfying all axioms for finite outcomes
- Proposes an innovative share-based mechanism for trading ratios of expected values
- Primary focus on the trade neutralization (TN) axiom: whether traders can sell contracts back for a nontrivial price
