# False Consensus, Information Theory, and Prediction Markets

## Abstract

The paper examines how Bayesian agents with shared priors make sequential public announcements based on private information. The researchers demonstrate that when agents' private information is independent conditioning on the event's outcome, whenever agents have similar beliefs about the outcome, their information is aggregated — indicating an absence of false consensus.

## Key Findings

- The analysis employs an information-theoretic framework centered on "interaction information," which measures shared information among three random variables
- The framework establishes an equivalence between interaction information's sign and whether information value is super- or sub-additive
- The approach enables reproving existing results, including Aaronson's 2005 work on agent agreement through belief announcements and Chen et al.'s 2010 findings on information release strategies in prediction markets
- The authors demonstrate that revealing information's expected reward equals the conditional mutual information of that information

The paper was accepted for publication at ITCS 2023.
