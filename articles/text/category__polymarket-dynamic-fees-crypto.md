# Polymarket Introduces Dynamic Fees to Combat Latency Arbitrage

## Overview

Polymarket has implemented a dynamic taker-fee model specifically for its 15-minute cryptocurrency markets. This structural change targets automated trading strategies that exploited pricing delays between Polymarket and major crypto exchanges.

## The Arbitrage Problem

Previously, under a zero-fee structure, sophisticated bots identified a repeatable profit opportunity. These automated systems monitored timing differences between Polymarket's internal pricing and spot prices on external exchanges. They executed trades when odds approached 50/50, then exited moments later once prices converged.

On-chain evidence suggests at least one wallet completed thousands of such trades in a single month with exceptionally high success rates, capturing consistent gains without directional risk exposure.

## Fee Structure Details

The new framework implements fees exclusively on takers executing against existing liquidity in 15-minute crypto markets. The fee scales with odds proximity to 50%, reaching approximately 3.15% on 50-cent contracts at that exact level. This pricing directly targets where latency-driven strategies operated most profitably.

The collected fees fund Polymarket's Maker Rebates Program, redistributing daily to liquidity providers. This incentivizes deeper order books and narrower spreads.

## Broader Platform Impact

Most Polymarket markets remain fee-free, including deposits, withdrawals, and longer-dated contracts. The change reflects market maturation, prioritizing quality over raw trading volume while closing early infrastructure inefficiencies.

## Implication for Hourly BTC Markets

The dynamic fee model at 15-minute markets also sets context for hourly BTC markets. Cross-platform arbitrage (Polymarket vs. Kalshi) remains viable on hourly markets since those have different fee structures, but the highest-frequency latency plays at 15-minute intervals are now economics-negative for most strategies.
