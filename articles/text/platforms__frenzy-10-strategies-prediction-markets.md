# Trading Strategies for Prediction Markets

Source: Frenzy Capital / Medium. Synthesizes 20 academic studies (2006–2026) into ten empirically-grounded trading strategies for prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket).

## Strategy 1: Favourite-Longshot Bias (2–5% edge)
Low-probability contracts are systematically overpriced due to "lottery ticket" appeal. Buy underpriced favourites above $0.50 and fade overpriced longshots by purchasing the corresponding "No" contracts on platforms like Kalshi.

## Strategy 2: Hedge Underpricing (3–15% edge)
Participants undervalue hedging instruments by 40–50%. When YES + NO prices sum to less than $1.00 on platforms with independent order books, buying both sides locks in risk-free profit.

## Strategy 3: Informed Trader Flow Signals (2–5% edge)
Sharp price movements in the final 30–60 minutes before resolution reflect superior information and are strongly predictive. Volume confirmation strengthens the signal.

## Strategy 4: Multi-Platform Arbitrage (1–5% edge)
Mathematically guaranteed profits emerge when the same event's prices across different platforms create opportunities where "buying both sides" costs less than $1.00 total payout.

## Strategy 5: Long-Horizon Forecasting (5–15% improvement)
Prediction markets outperformed election polls 74% of the time, with the strongest advantage 100+ days before events. Markets also predicted scientific replication failures 60–90 days in advance.

## Strategy 6: Price Interpretation at Extremes (2–10% correction)
Contracts below $0.15 or above $0.85 should be adjusted toward 50% by 3–8 percentage points due to risk-averse seller behaviour.

## Strategy 7: Political Connection Trading (1–2.5% abnormal returns)
Prediction market probability shifts precede stock market moves in politically-connected firms by 48–72 hours, creating a tradeable lag.

## Strategy 8: Polymarket Intra-Market & Combinatorial Arbitrage (Risk-free 2–60% per dollar)
Within Polymarket, YES + NO deviations and dependent NegRisk markets created approximately $40 million in extracted profit. Requires on-chain execution.

## Strategy 9: Kalshi Liquidity Provision (Variable; ~$29M aggregate across NFL season)
Passive liquidity providers function as underwriters rather than market makers, profiting from one-sided retail flow by quoting wider on concentrated demand.

## Strategy 10: Dynamic Hedging (Variable; locks profit on odds movement)
Close winning positions before resolution to capture guaranteed returns regardless of outcome. Requires identifying temporary price displacements and managing liquidity risk.

## Key Platform Distinctions
- **Kalshi**: CFTC-regulated, binary YES/NO contracts, no short selling (use "No" for bearish exposure), fixed pricing $0.01–$0.99, no margin risk.
- **Polymarket**: Order-book model, independent YES/NO trading, on-chain execution, supports combinatorial positions via NegRisk markets.

## Critical Caveats
The piece stresses liquidity risk (thin order books), resolution ambiguity, regulatory variation by jurisdiction, platform solvency risk, and edge decay as markets mature. Fees compound on multi-leg strategies.
