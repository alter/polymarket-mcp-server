# Exploring Decentralized Prediction Markets: Accuracy, Skill, and Bias on Polymarket

**Authors:** Felix Reichenbach, Martin Walther  
**Published:** December 2025 on SSRN  
**Coverage:** November 2022 – September 2025 (Polymarket)

## Scale

- **124 million+ trades** analyzed
- **68,000 different prediction markets**
- **Total volume:** USDC 48 billion (across all markets)
- Significant activity increase in 2024, particularly during the presidential election campaign

## Market-Level Findings

**No general longshot bias:** At the market level, researchers found a tendency to overtrade the default "Yes" option, but no evidence of a general longshot bias.

**Overall calibration:** Market prices closely track realized probabilities and slightly outperform bookmaker odds.

## Individual Trader-Level Findings

**Profitable traders:** Only **30% of traders earn positive profits**, and this share decreases over time.

**Skill evidence:** The proportion of traders with positive gains per bet exceeds expectations under randomness, and traders' profits are persistent over time — suggesting genuine skill exists among a subset.

**Exploitation:** Skilled traders may generate profits by exploiting the biases of less skilled market participants.

**Timing of errors:** Deviations from accuracy primarily arise in early trades and trades executed close to event resolution, indicating markets require time to process and incorporate new information.

## Key Implication

The existence of skill and bias at the individual level, while markets appear well-calibrated at the aggregate level, suggests a mechanism where:
1. Skilled traders identify and exploit mispricings created by biased traders
2. This arbitrage activity pushes prices toward accuracy
3. The market-level calibration masks significant individual heterogeneity

This is consistent with the London Business School / Yale finding that approximately 3% of traders drive most price discovery.
