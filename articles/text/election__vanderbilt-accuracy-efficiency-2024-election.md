# Prediction Markets? The Accuracy and Efficiency of $2.4 Billion in the 2024 Presidential Election

**Authors:** Joshua D. Clinton (Vanderbilt University), TzuFeng Huang  
**Published via:** SocArXiv / Center for Open Science, January 2025

## Abstract

The researchers examined over 2,500 political prediction markets traded across the Iowa Electronic Markets, Kalshi, PredictIt, and Polymarket during the final five weeks of the 2024 U.S. presidential campaign, involving more than $2 billion in transactions, to assess whether prices accurately and efficiently aggregate political information.

## Key Findings

### Accuracy by Platform
- **PredictIt:** 93% accuracy rate
- **Kalshi:** 78% accuracy rate
- **Polymarket:** 67% accuracy rate (lowest despite being the largest)

### Efficiency Concerns
Even the most accurate markets showed little evidence of efficiency:
- Prices for identical contracts diverged across exchanges
- Daily price changes were weakly correlated or negatively autocorrelated
- Arbitrage opportunities peaked in the final two weeks before Election Day

### Polymarket Specific Problems
- Polymarket's lack of betting limits allowed for significant price manipulation and "irrational movements"
- Mutually exclusive outcomes sometimes moved in the same direction simultaneously, indicating fundamental pricing inefficiencies
- Price volatility actually increased in the final two weeks before the election — when information should have been most abundant
- The platform "encourages herd behavior driven by visibility and hype rather than news"
- One trader (French Whale) held over 20% of shares predicting Trump victory; largest opposing position held only 7%

### Cross-Platform Market Quality
Clinton emphasized the study's deeper insight: "Similar markets were not only consistently priced differently, but also that the changes in daily closing prices were largely unrelated." This suggests traders reacted to each other rather than new information.

## Why PredictIt Outperformed

PredictIt's success is attributed to its structure with an $850 cap (since raised to $3,500) on individual bets that discouraged massive market-moving "whale" positions, instead favoring a larger number of smaller, more deliberate participants who were often deeply informed about specific local or niche races.

## Broader Implications

The research challenges the view that prediction markets necessarily efficiently and accurately aggregate information about political outcomes. The difference between "sober" analysis and "speculative" momentum creates fundamental tension in prediction markets.

Together, these findings challenge the view that prediction markets necessarily efficiently and accurately aggregate information about political outcomes.
