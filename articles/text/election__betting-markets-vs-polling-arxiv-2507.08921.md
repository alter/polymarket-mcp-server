# Are Betting Markets Better than Polling in Predicting Political Elections?

**Authors:** Laurie E. Cutting, Sarah S. Hughes-Berheim, Paul M. Johnson, Hiba Baroud, Brett Goldstein  
**ArXiv ID:** 2507.08921  
**Submitted:** July 11, 2025

## Abstract

The 2024 U.S. Presidential Election brought prediction markets into the mainstream spotlight. Polymarket, a blockchain-based prediction market platform, attracted unprecedented public and media attention, sparking debate over whether market prices or polling averages provided a more reliable guide to the election outcome. The researchers investigated whether data from Polymarket, an online betting platform, provides superior predictive insights compared to traditional polling methods for elections.

## Key Findings

The study concludes that "Polymarket was superior to polling in predicting the outcome of the 2024 presidential election, particularly in swing states." This aligns with established "Wisdom of Crowds" theory, suggesting large groups can accurately predict outcomes despite varying expertise levels.

**Swing State Performance:** Polymarket demonstrated superior predictive accuracy specifically in the battleground states that determined the electoral college outcome.

**Traditional Polling Failures:** In 2024, public polls again underestimated Donald Trump nationally and across major battleground states. States such as Iowa and New Jersey saw bigger polling misses too, with Trump outperforming final polling averages.

## Methodology

The researchers employed both descriptive and predictive analytics approaches. Rather than relying solely on voter preference questions typical in polls, they analyzed Polymarket data where participants wager money on expected outcomes — potentially capturing genuine expectations more effectively.

None of the prior research used Polymarket, the world's current largest prediction market run on cryptocurrency, which had close to $3.7 billion wagered on the 2024 presidential election.

## Caveats

Researchers were agnostic in their hypotheses, given that a small segment of the population that uses cryptocurrency and places bets using it is likely not a broad representation of the United States population at large.

Researchers were also cognizant that it would be critical to understand techniques to harden these markets ahead of future major events, given the sensitive nature of the data and its vulnerability to market manipulation.

## Conclusions

Overall, findings suggest that betting markets like Polymarket could be employed to predict presidential elections, but future investigations are needed to fully understand these results. Results are in alignment with research on "Wisdom of Crowds" theory, which suggests a large group of people are often accurate in predicting outcomes, even if they are not necessarily experts or closely aligned with the issue at hand.
