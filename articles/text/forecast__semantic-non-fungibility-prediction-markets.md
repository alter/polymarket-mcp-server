# Semantic Non-Fungibility and Violations of the Law of One Price in Prediction Markets

**Authors:** Jonas Gebele, Florian Matthes (Technical University of Munich)
**Date:** January 5, 2026
**arXiv:** 2601.01706v1

## Abstract

Examines how prediction markets across fragmented platforms fail to aggregate information globally. Despite listing "economically identical events," markets lack shared event identifiers, causing liquidity fragmentation and persistent price divergence. Constructed a human-validated cross-platform dataset spanning over 100,000 events and documented systematic 2–4% execution-aware price deviations even in highly liquid settings.

## Main Findings

**Cross-Platform Overlap:** Approximately 6% of events appear across multiple venues, concentrated in long-lived, high-salience events. Semantic-matching framework identifies 1,501 equivalence classes and 1,645 subset-related event sets.

**Price Divergence:** Maximum deviations remain substantial even at moderate liquidity levels. Median deviations persist at 2–4% from execution-adjusted parity, indicating "systematic directional disagreement" rather than transient noise.

**Arbitrage Persistence:** Execution costs fully eliminate only a subset of nominal price differences. Many opportunities exhibit "hundreds of percent annualized returns," though realized gains depend on market resolution horizons.

**2024 Election Case Study:** Even during the most liquid prediction-market event (>$4B volume), Polymarket–Kalshi prices diverged persistently despite common information and substantial overlapping participation.

## Conclusion

"Prediction markets fail to converge not because of insufficient information or incentives, but because they lack a shared, machine-verifiable language for contingent claims." Semantic non-fungibility — not transaction costs or latency — creates structural barriers to price alignment.

## Relevance to Prediction-Market Pricing

Identifies persistent cross-platform arbitrage opportunities between Kalshi and Polymarket. The 2–4% median price divergence represents systematic edge for cross-platform arbitrage strategies. Provides a dataset and methodology for building semantic matching between platforms.
