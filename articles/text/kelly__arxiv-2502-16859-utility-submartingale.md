# The Kelly Criterion And Utility Function Optimisation For Stochastic Binary Games: Submartingale And Supermartingale Regimes

**Author:** Steven D. Miller  
**arXiv ID:** 2502.16859  
**Submitted:** February 24, 2025  
**Length:** 26 pages  
**Subject area:** Probability Theory

## Abstract Overview

The paper presents a reformulation of the Kelly Criterion applied to stochastic Bernoulli binary games. The research examines how betting fractions affect wealth growth across repeated trials.

Key concepts include:
- Binary outcomes with probabilities p (win) and q (loss)
- A utility function defined as `E[log(W(N)/W(0))^(1/N)]` that optimizes through the Kelly fraction
- Analysis of submartingale and supermartingale regimes based on betting fraction thresholds

## Main Findings

The study demonstrates that the optimal Kelly fraction equals p − q (or 2p − 1), serving as "a critical point of U(F,p)" where U represents utility.

Key results include:
- Connections to Shannon entropy
- Mathematical characterization of wealth dynamics: when betting fractions fall **below** a critical threshold, wealth becomes a **submartingale** with positive utility
- When betting fractions exceed the critical threshold, wealth becomes a **supermartingale** with negative utility (overbetting regime)
- For large trial counts using the Kelly-optimal fraction, expected wealth exhibits exponential growth

## Relevance

This paper provides new formal proofs of the regimes (submartingale vs. supermartingale) that characterize the Kelly betting game, grounding the intuitive notion that overbetting destroys long-run wealth in rigorous probability theory.
