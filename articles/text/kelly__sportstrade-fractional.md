# The Fractional Kelly Bankroll Management System

## What Is the Kelly Criterion?

Named after John L. Kelly of AT&T Bell Laboratories, the Kelly Criterion is a formula designed to boost betting profits by determining the ideal percentage of capital to stake on each wagering event. Published in 1956 as "A New Interpretation of Information Rate."

The Kelly Criterion is now widely recognized as the "truest" sports betting bankroll management strategy. The core idea: bet more when your edge is large, less when it's small, and nothing when you have no edge.

## The Problem with Full Kelly

The formula can suggest staking a high percentage of your bankroll when you believe you have a significant edge, blindly following its recommendations can be risky. In some cases, the Kelly formula may suggest betting 20–40% of your bankroll on a single wager.

The basic Kelly formula also assumes you're betting sequentially, one bet at a time. In practice, you might have 10+ open bets at once. Full Kelly on each one can result in over 100% of your bankroll at risk.

## What Is Fractional Kelly?

Even Kelly supporters usually argue for fractional Kelly (betting a fixed fraction of the amount recommended by Kelly) for a variety of practical reasons, such as wishing to reduce volatility, or protecting against non-deterministic errors in their advantage (edge) calculations.

Usually, bettors applying the fractional Kelly criterion use half a Kelly, a quarter of a Kelly, or an eighth of a Kelly. If the amount of a bet according to the full Kelly criterion was 8% of the bankroll, the half, a quarter, and an eighth of Kelly bets would be 4%, 2%, and 1%, respectively.

## Growth vs. Risk Trade-Off

Half Kelly is the most popular choice among professionals — you get 75% of the growth rate of full Kelly with dramatically less variance, and your maximum drawdowns are roughly half what full Kelly would produce.

## Practical Variations

- **Fixed Kelly**: Setting a maximum percentage of the bankroll regardless of the calculated Kelly fraction
- **Averaged Kelly**: Using an average of recent Kelly calculations to smooth out fluctuations

## Key Recommendations

- The Kelly Criterion assumes you know the true probability of winning, which is extremely difficult in sports betting
- Most professionals use fractional Kelly (25% or less) to account for estimation errors and reduce variance
- Start with quarter Kelly and build confidence before increasing
