# Combinatorial Information Market Design (Hanson, 2003)

## Abstract

Information markets are markets created to aggregate information. Such markets usually estimate a probability distribution over the values of certain variables, via bets on those values. Combinatorial information markets would aggregate information on the entire joint probability distribution over many variables, by allowing bets on all variable value combinations.

## Key Findings

- Market scoring rules overcome the thin market and irrational participation problems that plague standard information markets
- Scoring rules avoid these problems but suffer from opinion pooling problems in the thick market case
- Market scoring rules avoid all these problems, by becoming automated market makers in the thick market case and simple scoring rules in the thin market case
- The most popular market scoring rule is the Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule (LMSR), known for least systematic bias in forecasts
- Introduces the foundational mechanism that underpins most modern automated prediction market makers

Published in Information Systems Frontiers 5, 107-119 (2003). DOI: 10.1023/A:1022058209073. This paper introduced LMSR, the dominant AMM mechanism for prediction markets.
