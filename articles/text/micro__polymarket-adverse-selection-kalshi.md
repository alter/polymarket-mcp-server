# Adverse Selection in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Kalshi

**Author:** Robert Bartlett (W. A. Franke Professor of Law and Business, Stanford)

**Published:** April 2026, SSRN

**Coverage link:** https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/21/adverse-selection-in-prediction-markets-evidence-from-kalshi/

## Abstract

Using 41.6 million trades from Kalshi prediction markets, this study adapts classical microstructure tools — Kyle's lambda, the Glosten-Harris decomposition, and VPIN — to binary contract prediction markets.

## Key Findings

1. **Single-name markets exhibit greater informed price impact than broad-based markets**, yet effective spreads are only modestly wider.

2. **Market makers earn twice as much per contract** in single-name markets — a puzzle resolved by behavioral cross-subsidy.

3. **Behavioral surplus:** traders systematically overbet YES in markets that predominantly settle NO, generating a surplus that cross-subsidizes adverse selection losses.

4. **One-sided order flow (VPIN-adapted) predicts maker losses** in single-name markets but not broad-based markets.

5. **New microstructure equilibrium concept** proposed for bilateral settlement markets (binary outcomes that sum to $1).

## Relevance to Polymarket CLOB Trading

- Direct application to Polymarket: same binary CLOB structure as Kalshi
- Confirms that YES-overbetting bias is systematic — exploitable as fade signal
- Adapted VPIN works on prediction market binary contracts — useful for toxicity detection
- Kyle's lambda measurably drops as market matures (see Polymarket 2024 election study)
- Spread decomposition applicable to Polymarket with modification for binary settlement mechanics
