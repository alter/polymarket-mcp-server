# Kalshi vs. CFTC: The Legal Battle Over Election Prediction Contracts

**Sources:** Multiple legal and news sources  
**Key Dates:** 2023–2025

## Background

Kalshi was established in 2018 by Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara. In November 2020, Kalshi attained a CFTC license, registering as a Designated Contract Market (DCM). The platform was publicly launched in July 2021.

## The Legal Battle

**June 2023:** Kalshi self-certified that its planned political event contracts complied with federal requirements.

**CFTC's Prohibition:** In a 3-2 vote, the CFTC determined political event contracts "involved gaming and unlawful activity" and prohibited them. Kalshi challenged this order in court.

**District Court Ruling:** The court found political event contracts did NOT "involve" gaming or unlawful activity because the underlying event (control of Congress) relates to politics and elections — not to gaming. The CFTC had "no authority to prohibit them."

**September 2024:** The US District Court for the District of Columbia ruled in favor of Kalshi in *Kalshi Inc. v. CFTC*.

**October 2024:** The D.C. Circuit court declined to stay the ruling, allowing election contracts to proceed.

**May 2025:** The CFTC voluntarily dismissed its appeal under the new Trump administration. Acting Chair Caroline Pham declared "the current Commission interpretations regarding event contracts are a sinkhole of legal uncertainty."

## Impact of the Ruling

- First legal regulated election trading for US citizens in over 100 years
- Kalshi received over **500 million site visits** during the 2024 election
- Raised over $500 million in presidential bets
- Expanded to sports, climate, economics, company events

## State-Level Pushback

Multiple states sent cease-and-desist letters claiming Kalshi violated state gaming laws:
- Nevada, New Jersey, Maryland — all sent cease-and-desist
- Kalshi sued all three states seeking federal preemption
- **Nevada and New Jersey:** Federal courts sided with Kalshi (federal preemption)
- **Maryland:** Court denied preliminary injunction; found CEA preemption insufficient

## Insider Trading Regulations

In response to Maduro/Venezuela betting scandal (2026), Kalshi suspended and fined three congressional candidates for "political insider trading" on their own campaigns.

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig announced in January 2026 the CFTC would move forward with a prediction markets rulemaking.

## Broader Legal Context

The law around prediction markets traces back to early gambling law. From the 1880s to 1930s, political betting was common in New York but was gradually restricted by the Hart-Agnew Act (1908/1910) and state anti-gambling legislation.

The fundamental question: Are prediction market event contracts federally regulated derivatives subject to CFTC exclusivity, or state-regulated gambling subject to traditional police powers?

More than 36 states filed amicus briefs asserting states' rights to regulate sports gambling in related litigation.
