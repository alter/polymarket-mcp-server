# Futarchy: Vote on Values, But Bet on Beliefs

**Concept by:** Robin Hanson  
**Overview article:** https://www.lesswrong.com/w/futarchy  
**Primary source:** Hanson, R. (2000). "Futarchy: Vote Values, But Bet Beliefs." Working paper.  
**Also:** Hanson, R. (1999). "Decision Markets." IEEE Intelligent Systems.

## Core Concept

Futarchy is a governance model proposed by economist Robin Hanson where elected officials define measures of national wellbeing, and prediction markets are used to determine which policies will have the most positive effect.

**Motto:** "Vote on Values, But Bet on Beliefs."

The term "futarchy" is a portmanteau of "future" and aspects of governance/markets.

## Historical Development

Robin Hanson first explored related concepts in his 1999 paper "Decision Markets," which proposed using speculative markets to estimate the consequences of policy decisions. In 2000, Hanson formalized futarchy in "Futarchy: Vote Values, But Bet Beliefs." It was named by The New York Times as a buzzword of 2008.

## How It Works

1. **Democratic Valuation (Stage 1):** Citizens vote on what metrics matter most—GDP growth, happiness levels, family stability—establishing an official welfare measure. Democracy defines welfare metrics.

2. **Market Implementation (Stage 2):** Betting markets predict policy outcomes. Traders buy and sell "shares" of policies based on expected welfare impacts. Policies with significantly higher estimated welfare gains get implemented.

3. **Conditional execution:** Policies get adopted if and only if decision markets imply that the policy is likely to improve a measure of national welfare.

## Decision Markets

Futarchy is an application of decision markets, which is an application of prediction markets in general. A decision market allows organizations to put up proposals for trading, and traders to express a view on what they think the impact is if the proposal passes or fails.

Building on the hypothesis that markets are better than human organizations at aggregating information, futarchy introduces the use of prediction markets as a tool for organizational decision-making.

## Expansion to Blockchain and DAOs

Vitalik Buterin's 2014 blog post "An Introduction to Futarchy" explored its application to decentralized governance in Ethereum-based DAOs. The concept has been expanded to explore its potential in the context of blockchains and DAOs.

## Key Challenges

- **Practical obstacles:** Despite decades of discussion, futarchy has seen limited large-scale implementation.
- **Correlation vs. causation:** Markets can conflate correlation with causation.
- **Measurement difficulties:** Creating objective welfare metrics is extremely challenging.
- **Wealth concentration:** Richer participants gain disproportionate influence over market prices.
- **Confounding variables:** Other events can affect welfare metrics simultaneously.

## Futarchy in Practice (as of 2026)

Hanson notes that futarchy is "now finally seeing some substantial trials, 25 years after its invention." Projects implementing aspects:
- **Zeitgeist** (Polkadot): First blockchain project implementing futarchy governance
- **Robin Markets:** Decision markets platform
- **Vitalik's proposals:** Two-layer Ethereum governance model using prediction markets
