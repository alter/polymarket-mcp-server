# Interpreting Prediction Market Prices as Probabilities (Wolfers & Zitzewitz, 2006)

## Abstract

While most empirical analysis of prediction markets treats prices of binary options as predictions of the probability of future events, Manski (2004) argued that there is little existing theory supporting this practice. This paper provides relevant analytic foundations, describing sufficient conditions under which prediction market prices correspond with mean beliefs.

## Key Findings

- For a broad class of models, prediction market prices are usually close to the mean beliefs of traders
- Prediction market prices typically serve as useful (albeit sometimes biased) estimates of average beliefs regarding event probabilities
- The key parameters driving trading behavior are the degree of risk aversion and the distribution of beliefs
- Risk aversion levels and the spread of trader beliefs significantly influence price-probability relationships
- The paper provides empirical data on belief distributions in several contexts
