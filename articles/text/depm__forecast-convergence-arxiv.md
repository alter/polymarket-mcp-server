# On convergence of forecasts in prediction markets

**Authors:** Nina Badulina, Dmitry Shatilovich, Mikhail Zhitlukhin  
**arXiv:** 2402.16345  
**Submitted:** February 26, 2024  
**Subject:** Probability (math.PR); Mathematical Finance (q-fin.MF)  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16345

## Abstract

The researchers propose "a dynamic model of a prediction market in which agents predict the values of a sequence of random vectors." Their central finding demonstrates that "if there are agents who make correct (or asymptotically correct) next-period forecasts, then the aggregated market forecasts converge to the next-period conditional expectations."

## Key Finding

When skilled forecasters participate in prediction markets—those providing accurate or nearly accurate predictions—the collective market forecast gravitates toward mathematically optimal predictions (conditional expectations) for upcoming periods.

This result provides theoretical grounding for the hypothesis that prediction markets efficiently aggregate information: if at least some participants have accurate beliefs, the market price will converge to the correct probability.

## Significance

- Provides mathematical proof for prediction market information aggregation
- Establishes conditions under which markets "discover" true probabilities
- Connects to broader literature on market efficiency and belief aggregation
- Relevant to evaluating Polymarket, Augur, and other DePMs as forecasting tools
