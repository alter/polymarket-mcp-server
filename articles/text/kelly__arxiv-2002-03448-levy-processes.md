# Kelly Criterion: From a Simple Random Walk to Lévy Processes

**Authors:** Sergey Lototsky and Austin Pollok  
**arXiv ID:** 2002.03448  
**Submitted:** February 9, 2020  
**Classification:** Mathematics > Probability (math.PR) and Mathematical Finance (q-fin.MF)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2002.03448

## Abstract

The original Kelly criterion provides a strategy to maximize the long-term growth of winnings in a sequence of simple Bernoulli bets with an edge, that is, when the expected return on each bet is positive. The research extends this foundational concept by examining "more general models of returns and the continuous time, or high frequency, limits of those models."

## Key Topics

- Extension of Kelly from simple Bernoulli bets to general stochastic processes
- Continuous-time and high-frequency limits
- Lévy processes as models for returns
- Connection to earlier work by Thorp, Merton, Latané, Hakansson

## Mathematical Subject Classifications

60G50, 60F05, 60F17, 60G51, 91A60

## Context

The Kelly criterion in continuous-time was studied in discrete time by Latané (1959) and Hakansson (1970), and in continuous time by Merton (1990) and Karatzas (1989). This paper extends the framework all the way to Lévy processes — a highly general class of stochastic processes that includes jumps, fat tails, and other features absent from Brownian motion, making the framework relevant to real-world markets with discontinuous price moves.
