# Transaction Fees and Optimal Rebalancing in the Growth-Optimal Portfolio

**Authors:** Yu Feng, Matus Medo, Liang Zhang, Yi-Cheng Zhang  
**arXiv ID:** 1009.3753 (q-fin.PM)  
**Submitted:** September 20, 2010; revised January 6, 2011  
**Published:** Physica A 390, 1635-1645 (2011)

## Abstract Summary

The paper examines how transaction fees impact the Kelly-inspired strategy of constant portfolio rebalancing. The researchers demonstrate that "fees may give rise to an optimal period of portfolio rebalancing." They analytically derive the optimal rebalancing period for lognormal returns, then generalize these findings across various return distributions and GARCH processes.

## Key Findings

1. **Optimal Rebalancing Intervals**: Rather than continuous rebalancing, transaction costs create incentives for periodic adjustments at specific intervals.
2. **Partial Rebalancing Benefits**: The study shows that "this strategy can improve the investment long-term growth rate more than optimization of the rebalancing period" when rebalancing occurs partially rather than completely.

## Relevance for Prediction Markets

Prediction market positions incur fees on entry and exit (Polymarket: 0.10%, Kalshi: variable). This paper provides the theoretical basis for determining optimal rebalancing frequency accounting for those fees, preventing the "churn" that degrades returns even with a genuine edge.
