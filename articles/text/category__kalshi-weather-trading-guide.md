# Kalshi Weather Market Trading Guide

## Overview
Kalshi provides daily weather prediction markets for four cities: New York City, Chicago, Miami, and Austin. Traders predict high temperatures as reported by the National Weather Service.

## Market Structure
Markets are organized into six temperature brackets. The four middle brackets span 2 degrees each, while the outer brackets capture all temperatures below or above specified thresholds. Markets launch at 10 AM the day before the prediction date.

## Starting Strategy
The guide recommends beginning with basic weather forecasts from established sources like the National Weather Service, Weather.com, and Apple Weather. As noted: "The markets are usually (but absolutely not always) structured so that the predicted high is in one of the center brackets."

## Advanced Approach
Beyond standard forecasts, traders should consult weather models that may diverge from predictions. Key models: GFS (Global Forecast System), ECMWF (Euro Model), NAM (North American Mesoscale). Use Ventusky to access all weather models in one module for comparing different modeling systems.

## Identifying Opportunities
Successful traders should:
- Identify patterns when forecasts consistently miss (such as hazy conditions affecting actual temperatures)
- Monitor real-time temperature progression throughout trading days
- Purchase undervalued shares in legitimate bracket possibilities

## Important Considerations
Traders must understand specific resolution details: exact airport locations (Midway, LaGuardia, etc.), how Daylight Saving Time applies, and where official temperatures are first reported.

## Key Competitive Dynamic
Kalshi weather markets are not populated by casual bettors — they're populated by people who watch the NWS forecast model cycle run at 6 AM, 12 PM, and 6 PM, and execute trades within seconds of an update. Fee structure: flat per contract. On a $0.05 contract, fees represent a 20% immediate cost. Rule of thumb: never trade contracts below $0.15.
