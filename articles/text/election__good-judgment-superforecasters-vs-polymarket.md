# Testing Polymarket's "Most Accurate" Claim: Superforecasters vs. Prediction Markets

**Source:** Good Judgment Inc. / Substack  
**Date:** 2025

## Overview

Good Judgment Inc. (the company founded by Philip Tetlock and co-founded by Barbara Mellers) conducted a rigorous head-to-head comparison between Polymarket prediction markets and professional Superforecasters, using central bank rate decisions as a testing ground.

## Methodology

- **Test domain:** 25 recent monetary policy meetings (Federal Reserve, European Central Bank, Bank of England, Bank of Japan)
- **Outcomes:** Three mutually exclusive outcomes — raise, hold, or cut
- **Metric:** Brier score (lower = better)
- Forecasts aligned on the same questions, evaluated on the same timeline

## Results

**Superforecasters won on 76% of forecast days.**

| Metric | Polymarket | Superforecasters |
|---|---|---|
| Average daily Brier score | 0.159 | **0.135** |
| Days with better score | 24% | **76%** |
| Approximate accuracy gap | | **~18–24% better** |

## Implications

The evidence shows that when forecasting systems are evaluated head-to-head on the same questions using standard accuracy metrics, the Superforecasters' aggregate forecast performs better than prediction markets in this domain — undercutting claims of universal predictive supremacy for Polymarket.

## Context

This does not mean Polymarket is useless — it excels in high-liquidity political markets with real-time information updating advantages. But the narrative that prediction markets have replaced all other forecasting methods is not supported by rigorous comparison.

## Historical Background

Tetlock's IARPA forecasting tournament found that superforecasters matched or slightly exceeded prediction market accuracy — but only after extensive training and team aggregation. The average untrained forecaster lagged behind liquid prediction markets.

The Good Judgment finding is consistent with: Superforecasting as a methodology can outperform open prediction markets in structured head-to-head tests, particularly in domains where expert knowledge and calibrated probabilistic thinking are more valuable than crowd aggregation.
