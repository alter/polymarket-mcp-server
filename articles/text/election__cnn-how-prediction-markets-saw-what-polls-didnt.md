# How Prediction Markets Saw Something the Polls and Pundits Didn't

**Source:** CNN Business  
**Date:** November 8, 2024

## Overview

Post-election analysis by CNN examining how prediction markets — particularly Polymarket and Kalshi — correctly forecast Trump's victory when conventional polls showed a tight race.

## Key Facts

- While major polling models described the Trump-Harris race as a 50/50 "toss-up" until the final hours of Election Night, prediction markets consistently priced a Trump victory at roughly **60/40** throughout October 2024
- By the time the Associated Press officially called the race, prediction markets had been trading at **95% certainty** for hours
- Polymarket had Trump's odds at **95%+ before midnight** of election day, several hours before the election was called

## The Selzer Miss

Just days before the election, legendary pollster Ann Selzer released a poll showing Kamala Harris leading by **3 points in Iowa** — a state Trump had won handily in 2016 and 2020. This sent shockwaves through traditional media. The prediction markets largely shrugged it off, maintaining Trump's lead. Trump ultimately won Iowa by **14 points**, marking one of the most significant misses in modern polling history.

## Market vs. Poll Divergence

On the Monday before the election, Trump led Kamala Harris **58% to 42%** on Polymarket — a lead that proved to be a much more accurate reflection of reality.

When the Selzer Iowa poll was released showing Harris doing better than expected, "Trump's election odds tumbled on betting markets" briefly — then recovered, suggesting markets treated it as noise while maintaining their underlying Trump-favorable position.

## Nate Silver's Observation

Nate Silver, serving as a Polymarket advisor, said one notable shift in Trump's favor was a "larger swing than is justified" — acknowledging that even he was skeptical of the magnitude while the market maintained its position.

## Context: The French Whale

The market's pro-Trump stance was partly driven by a single French trader who placed $80 million in bets on Trump, ultimately winning $85 million. While some suspected manipulation, Polymarket found no evidence of it — and the bet proved correct.

## Broader Significance

CNN's analysis suggested this was the first election cycle where prediction market odds received near-mainstream-media-equivalent coverage, with market prices regularly cited alongside polling averages in political coverage. The post-election narrative of prediction markets "getting it right" when polls failed accelerated media integration of prediction market data.
