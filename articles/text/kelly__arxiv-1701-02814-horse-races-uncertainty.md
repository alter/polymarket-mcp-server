# Kelly Betting on Horse Races with Uncertainty in Probability Estimates

**Author:** Michael R. Metel  
**arXiv ID:** 1701.02814 [math.OC]  
**Submitted:** January 11, 2017; revised August 2, 2017

## Abstract

The paper examines optimal betting strategies when outcome probabilities contain estimation errors. The research "investigate[s] the problem of gambling with uncertainty in outcome probabilities" through stochastic optimization approaches applied to events with mutually exclusive outcomes.

## Key Content

The work focuses on probability estimation using multinomial logistic regression for betting decisions. The author gives particular emphasis to scenarios with two outcomes as well as more complex multi-outcome situations.

An empirical component using simulated data demonstrates quantifiable losses resulting from probability estimation inaccuracy. Importantly, the study shows that "superior returns are achieved taking it into consideration" — meaning accounting for uncertainty in probability estimates yields better betting performance than ignoring such estimation errors.

## Contributions

- Convex optimization models for Kelly betting considering probability estimation uncertainty from multinomial logistic regression
- The greatest returns were achieved using a robust optimization approach, which also resulted in a significant reduction in wagering compared to standard Kelly betting
- Provides formal analysis of how estimation error degrades Kelly performance

## Relevance

This paper is directly applicable to prediction market trading, where probability estimates are always imperfect. The robust approach suggested here — accounting for uncertainty in one's probability estimates — is a formal alternative to the simpler "use fractional Kelly" heuristic.
