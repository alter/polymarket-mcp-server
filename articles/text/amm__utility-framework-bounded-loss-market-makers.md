# A Utility Framework for Bounded-Loss Market Makers

**Authors:** Yiling Chen, David M. Pennock

**arXiv ID:** 1206.5252 | Published at UAI 2007

## Abstract

The authors introduce utility-based market makers that always accept orders at their risk-neutral prices. They establish conditions ensuring bounded losses and prove equivalences between three formulations: hyperbolic absolute risk aversion (HARA) utilities, weighted pseudospherical scoring rules, and cost functions. The framework shows that Hanson's logarithmic scoring rule market maker corresponds to a negative exponential utility market maker. The research explores liquidity-loss tradeoffs, demonstrating that while different market makers excel at different price ranges, none dominates uniformly across all scenarios.

## Key Contributions

- Derives necessary and sufficient conditions for bounded-loss market makers
- Establishes equivalence between three market maker formulations: HARA utility, scoring rule, cost function
- LMSR = negative exponential utility market maker (key insight)
- Introduces the constant logarithmic utility market maker (CLUM)
- Analyzes liquidity-loss tradeoffs across price regimes
- Provides lower bounds on worst-case losses given minimum liquidity requirements

## Significance

Foundational paper establishing the utility-function interpretation of market scoring rules. The equivalence between LMSR and negative exponential utility underpins much of the subsequent mechanism design work.
