# Explaining the Favorite-Longshot Bias

**Journal:** *Journal of Political Economy*, Vol 118, No 4  
**NBER Working Paper:** w15923  
**URL:** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/655844

## What is the Favorite-Longshot Bias?

In gambling and economics: bettors tend to **overvalue longshots** and **undervalue favorites**. First discovered by Griffith; documented across horse racing, soccer, basketball, tennis for decades.

Core empirical regularity: market odds are NOT unbiased estimates of true probabilities — longshot bets lose more than favorite bets on average.

## Competing Explanations

### 1. Risk-Love (Neoclassical)
Rational gamblers overbet longshots because they have **convex utility functions** — they prefer small probability of large gain (lottery-like payoff). Supported by earlier horse racing literature.

### 2. Probability Misperception (Behavioral)
Bettors **overestimate the likelihood of rare events** (longshots occurring). This is a cognitive bias — systematically miscalibrated probability assessments. Supported by this paper's analysis.

## Key Finding

"An expected utility model and a prospect theory model confirm that the favourite-longshot bias on the betting exchange is due to **misperception of probabilities** rather than risk-love."

Evidence for misperception over risk-love:
- Bias is present on betting EXCHANGES (Betfair) where both back and lay positions available
- If it were pure risk-love, rational traders would exploit the bias by laying longshots
- The persistence on exchanges implies participants don't arbitrage it away — suggesting systematic misperception

## Market Structure Explanation

**Bookmaker risk aversion in fixed-odds markets:** When gamblers disagree, bookmakers set prices to balance their book exposure. Disagreement among bettors + risk-averse bookmakers = persistent favorite-longshot bias in fixed-odds markets.

Formula:
- If 65% of money backs the longshot (overbet), bookmaker shortens the longshot price to balance exposure
- This creates favorable odds for favorites relative to true probability

## Empirical Magnitude

In horse racing: favorites lose ~5% on average vs. longshots losing ~40% on average. The magnitude of the bias grows with longshot odds — the more extreme the longshot, the worse the expected return.

## Transferable Edges

1. **Systematically fade longshots** across all betting/prediction markets
2. Betting exchanges (Betfair, Polymarket orderbook) still exhibit this bias despite two-sided market structure
3. The bias is larger for extreme longshots ($0.05–$0.15) and extreme favorites ($0.85–$0.95)
4. Misperception is cognitive and persistent — it doesn't arbitrage away easily
5. On Kalshi: takers at 5-cent contracts win only 4.18% when implied probability is 5% — confirming this bias transfers directly
