# Iowa Electronic Markets: Forecasting the 2024 US Presidential Election

**Source:** PS: Political Science & Politics, Cambridge Core  
**Published:** Late 2024

## What is the Iowa Electronic Markets?

The Iowa Electronic Markets (IEM) are a collection of real-money prediction markets operated by the Henry B. Tippie College of Business at the University of Iowa as an educational and research project. Unlike commercial prediction markets, the IEM is not-for-profit. Traders can invest between $5.00 and $500.00.

Two types of markets:
- **Vote-Share (VS) markets:** Forecast the vote shares taken by each candidate
- **Winner-Takes-All (WTA) markets:** Forecast the probability that each candidate will receive the most popular votes

## 2024 Presidential Election Forecasts

**As of September 29, 2024:**
- Vote-share market: 9.0-percentage-point Democratic margin (54.5% to 45.5%)
- Winner-takes-all market: **85.7% chance** of Democratic popular vote win
- Combined forecast: 6–7 percentage point Democratic margin, **87.0% probability** of Democratic win

**As of October 17, 2024:**
- Vote-share market: Harris 53%, Trump 48.1%
- Winner-takes-all market: Harris **89.4%**, Trump **12.2%**

**Outcome:** These forecasts were **significantly wrong** — Donald Trump won the presidency with 295 Electoral College votes to Harris's 226, and also won the popular vote. This is a notable failure of the IEM's 2024 prediction.

## Historical Context

**Long-term track record:**
- Average absolute prediction error: 1.34 percentage points
- Outperforms polls 74% of the time overall
- Significantly outperforms polls in every election when forecasting more than 100 days in advance
- Berg, Nelson, and Rietz (2008) compared IEM to 964 polls over five presidential elections

**Debate Impact:**
The probability of a Democratic popular vote win fell from 70–80% range to 59% following the June 27 debate, remaining below 70% for nearly a month.

**Candidate Uncertainty:**
Between June 27 and August 6, approximately 46% of daily IEM distributions were bimodal, reflecting two competing scenarios.

## Key Takeaway

Despite its strong historical track record, the IEM significantly miscalled the 2024 presidential election, consistently projecting an 85–89% probability of a Democratic win right up to the election. This contrasted sharply with Polymarket, which showed Trump as the likely winner. The 2024 IEM failure illustrates that even long-established, well-respected prediction markets can systematically err — particularly when the participating pool is small and demographically narrow.
