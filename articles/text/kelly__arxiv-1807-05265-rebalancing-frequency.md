# Rebalancing Frequency Considerations for Kelly-Optimal Stock Portfolios in a Control-Theoretic Framework

**Authors:** Chung-Han Hsieh, John A. Gubner, B. Ross Barmish  
**arXiv ID:** 1807.05265  
**Submitted:** July 13, 2018 (revised August 21, 2018)  
**Venue:** IEEE Conference on Decision and Control, Miami Beach, FL, 2018

## Abstract Overview

The authors investigate portfolio optimization following Kelly's framework while focusing on how often traders should adjust their holdings. Their central research question: "does high-frequency trading always lead to the best performance?" when transaction costs are absent.

## Key Findings

- "There are no performance benefits associated with high-frequency trading" in many scenarios
- The researchers introduce a "dominance" condition: when satisfied by an asset, the optimal strategy concentrates entirely in that single holding, making rebalancing frequency irrelevant
- The work extends previous analysis from two-asset portfolios to multi-asset scenarios and includes simulations reflecting real market conditions

## Significance

Contrary to intuition, constant portfolio rebalancing doesn't necessarily maximize expected logarithmic growth rates. This has direct implications for prediction market traders wondering how often to size up / size down their positions.
