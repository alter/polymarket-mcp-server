# Weather Forecasts and Weather Derivatives: Mathematical Framework

arXiv:physics/0312125

## Core Concepts

The paper explores how weather forecast data can be modeled as stochastic processes, specifically using Brownian motion frameworks. The research investigates whether temperature forecasts exhibit properties consistent with random walk behavior.

## Key Mathematical Framework

- **Feynmann-Kac formula**: Used to relate differential equations to expectations under stochastic processes
- **Fokker-Planck equation**: Governs probability density evolution for weather variables
- **Black-Scholes methodology**: Adapted from equity options to price weather-based derivatives

The paper demonstrates that "weather derivative fair prices are martingales," meaning their expected values remain constant under risk-neutral measures.

## Methodology

The research analyzes statistical properties of weather forecasts, examining forecast volatility and reliability over different time horizons. It establishes PDEs (partial differential equations) governing fair pricing for weather derivatives through arbitrage-free pricing arguments.

## Key Finding

By connecting weather forecast uncertainty to classical financial mathematics, the work provides theoretical justification for applying option-pricing models to meteorological derivative contracts. This bridges quantitative meteorology and quantitative finance.

The paper includes extensive citations to foundational works in stochastic processes, financial derivatives, and weather modeling literature.

## Relevance to Prediction Markets

This mathematical framework underpins modern weather derivative pricing and is relevant to prediction market weather contracts. The martingale property means that in a well-functioning weather prediction market, current prices should reflect all available forecast information, and systematic trading edges must come from information not yet incorporated into prices.
