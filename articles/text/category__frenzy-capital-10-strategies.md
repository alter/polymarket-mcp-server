# Trading Strategies for Prediction Markets

This Medium article by Frenzy Capital synthesizes 20 academic studies into 10 empirically-grounded trading strategies for prediction markets.

## The Ten Strategies

### 1. Favourite-Longshot Bias (2–5% edge)
Contracts on unlikely outcomes are systematically overpriced while favorites are underpriced. Strategy: buying "No" contracts on overpriced longshots or buying Yes contracts on underpriced favorites, particularly when prices fall between $0.05–$0.15 or $0.75–$0.92.

### 2. Hedge Underpricing (3–15% edge)
Participants undervalue hedging instruments by 40–50%. Traders can exploit this by buying complementary Yes/No pairs when their combined price falls below $1.00, or by cross-platform hedging where one platform prices protection cheaper than another.

### 3. Informed Trader Flow Signals (2–5% edge)
Large informed bets cluster in final 30–60 minutes before resolution. Traders following sharp probability shifts accompanied by above-average volume capture predictive signals from sophisticated participants.

### 4. Multi-Platform Arbitrage (1–5% edge)
Risk-free profits emerge when the same event trades at different prices across platforms. Buying the cheapest Yes and cheapest No across different venues yields guaranteed returns when combined cost falls below $1.00.

### 5. Long-Horizon Forecasting (5–15% edge)
Prediction markets outperform polls for events 100+ days out. Early positioning based on historical base rates rather than current market prices captures the market's superior accuracy at distant horizons.

### 6. Price Interpretation at Extremes (2–10% edge)
Contracts priced near $0.00 or $1.00 reflect mean participant beliefs rather than true probabilities. A $0.10 contract likely represents 13–15% true probability, not 10%.

### 7. Political Connection Trading (1–2.5% edge)
Stock prices of firms connected to political candidates lag prediction market price changes by 2–3 days. Traders identifying these connections and trading equities after prediction market shifts capture the lag.

### 8. Polymarket Intra-Market Arbitrage (5–60% edge)
Within Polymarket, YES and NO prices should sum to $1.00. When they don't, buying both sides captures risk-free profit. Related "NegRisk" markets provide combinatorial arbitrage. Approximately $40M extracted through this mechanism documented.

### 9. Kalshi Liquidity Provision (variable edge)
Passive liquidity providers on Kalshi function as underwriters rather than market makers. They absorb one-sided retail flow and profit from directional terminal exposure. Approximately $29M aggregate profit across one NFL season from this strategy.

### 10. Dynamic Hedging (variable edge)
Traders open positions when believing prices are displaced from fair value, then close by taking the opposite side when odds move favorably, locking in guaranteed profit regardless of outcome.

## Key Insights

"The favourite-longshot bias is the single most robust finding in prediction market research" spanning decades. Arbitrage opportunities narrow as markets mature and attract sophistication.

Platform mechanics differ significantly: Kalshi uses separate Yes/No contracts with CFTC regulation; Polymarket uses order-book trading with independent Yes/No shares; traditional sportsbooks lack short-selling mechanisms entirely.

## Cross-Category Applicability

These strategies apply across all Polymarket categories: weather, sports, crypto, and macro/economic markets. The specific calibration biases noted in the domain-specific calibration paper (arxiv 2602.19520) affect which strategy is most appropriate per category.
