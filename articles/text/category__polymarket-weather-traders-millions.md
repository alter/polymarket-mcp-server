# Traders on Polymarket Earn Millions by Predicting Weather

## Overview
Sophisticated traders have discovered profitable opportunities on Polymarket by predicting weather outcomes with data-driven strategies. Two notable traders exemplify different approaches to this emerging market niche.

## Trader Profiles and Strategies

### Neobrother: The Systematic Predictor
This trader has accumulated over $20,000 in profits by focusing exclusively on weather predictions, particularly daily high temperatures in major cities. His approach employs a **temperature laddering strategy** — simultaneously placing bets across multiple temperature points (29°C through 34°C+) with minimal individual costs ranging from 0.2¢ to 15¢.

The strategy functions similarly to grid trading in traditional finance. By covering a probable temperature range densely, even if most positions lose, significant returns from one or two winning positions offset losses. For example, one Buenos Aires 31°C position generated an **811.78% return**.

### Hans323: The Asymmetric Odds Specialist
This trader employed a contrasting high-conviction approach, securing a **$1.11 million profit** from a single **$92,632 wager** on a low-probability London weather event carrying only an 8% winning chance. His strategy mirrors leveraged hedge fund tactics — accepting numerous small losses in exchange for massive returns when rare events occur.

Specific tactics:
- Buying YES shares priced below $0.15
- Buying NO shares priced above $0.45
- Limiting risk to approximately $1 per position
- Repeating across thousands of trades

## Key Success Factors

Both traders likely possess advantages including:
- Superior weather model analysis (ECMWF, GFS)
- Real-time data access
- Automated trading systems

Weather markets represent "pure physics and math," offering profit opportunities to analysts with accurate predictive models.

## Airport vs. City-Center Gap

Every Polymarket temperature market resolves at a specific airport station. Most retail traders use city-center weather readings as their reference. The gap between city center and airport temperature is not random noise — it has a predictable sign and magnitude based on local geography, creating a reliable edge component when the pattern is clear (e.g., confirmed sea-breeze day in NYC, confirmed heat dome with light winds in Paris).
