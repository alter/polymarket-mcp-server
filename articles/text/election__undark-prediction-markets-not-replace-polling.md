# Prediction Markets Aren't Likely to Replace Polling

**Source:** Undark  
**Date:** May 5, 2026

## Overview

Prediction markets like Polymarket and Kalshi have gained prominence following the 2024 U.S. presidential election, where they accurately forecast a Trump victory. However, experts argue these markets fundamentally differ from traditional polling and cannot serve as replacements.

## Key Differences Between Markets and Polls

Polls measure public sentiment at specific moments, while prediction markets involve wagering on future outcomes.

Courtney Kennedy from the Pew Research Center emphasizes: "I never use prediction markets to try to understand public opinion" because they rely on "people's guesses as to what that is" rather than data from the actual public.

Christopher Wlezien, a University of Texas political scientist, notes the comparison is fundamentally flawed: "I'm a big fan of prediction markets, I use them, but these comparisons are kind of silly." He distinguishes them as "a forecasting device with a preference-registering device."

## Performance Analysis

Historical research shows mixed results. Wlezien's 2012 analysis found that "following the advent of opinion polling based on statistical methods in the 1930s, election markets performed no better than polls as election predictors."

Recent 2025 research by Rajiv Sethi analyzing the 2024 Trump-Harris race found prediction markets performed comparably to statistical models but "fared particularly poorly down the ballot compared to the models, especially for Congressional races."

## Market Biases and Concerns

Research identifies several vulnerabilities:

- **Demographic skew:** Traders are "predominantly young and male," affecting price accuracy
- **Whale influence:** Single large traders can manipulate outcomes (the "Romney Whale" wagered nearly $4 million in 2012; French Whale $80M in 2024)
- **Wash trading:** "Trading with yourself" to artificially inflate volumes, particularly common on crypto-based platforms. Columbia study found ~25% of Polymarket volume may be wash trades.
- **Insider trading:** Cases involve bets placed before military actions (Maduro operation, Israeli strikes)

## Regulatory and Ethical Issues

Markets face criticism for profiting from conflict. Regulators view them more as gambling platforms than financial exchanges. Concerns include potential market manipulation, lack of standardized uncertainty estimates, and implications for political behavior.

## Expert Perspectives

Rajiv Sethi suggests markets excel during unprecedented times: "My view of prediction markets is that they have the potential to generate valuable forecasts for certain events, especially when we're in uncharted waters and history is not a good guide to the future."

However, Sethi cautions that the Trump-Harris success represents "merely a single data point," and broader assessment shows "the jury is still out on the accuracy of markets versus models."

## Ethical Distinction

Kennedy emphasizes a crucial distinction: "Polls are not profiting from human suffering or political instability or anything like that. Polls are designed to increase understanding; it's not about anybody making a profit."
