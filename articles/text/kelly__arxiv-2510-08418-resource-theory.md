# A Resource Theory of Gambling

**Authors:** Maite Arcos, Renato Renner, Jonathan Oppenheim  
**arXiv ID:** 2510.08418 (quant-ph)  
**Submitted:** October 9, 2025  
**Length:** 42 pages  
**Subjects:** Quantum Physics; Computer Science and Game Theory

## Abstract Summary

The researchers develop a framework treating betting games as a "resource theory of adversarial information." They extend the classical Kelly criterion — traditionally understood in the context of repeated bets — to single-shot and finite-betting scenarios.

Key contributions include:
- Computing optimal betting strategies that maximize success probability for reaching a target return rate within a finite number of bets
- Revealing a risk-reward tradeoff characterized by Rényi divergences between the true probability distribution and the odds-maker's odds
- Demonstrating that one-shot optimal strategies align with expected utility maximization and hypothesis testing error minimization
- Extending the framework to distributed games where multiple players observe correlated information about unknown states
- Generalizing to quantum domains where "quantum side-information and entanglement play analogous roles"

## Key Insight

The paper "bridges economic and information-theoretic viewpoints" by recasting gambling as an adversarial resource theory, offering a unified perspective applicable to both classical and quantum settings.

## Significance

This paper resolves a fundamental gap in Kelly theory: classical Kelly is optimal only in the limit of infinitely many bets. This work derives what is optimal for a **finite** number of bets using Rényi divergences — important for real-world prediction market traders who have limited capital and finite numbers of trades.
