# Semantic Non-Fungibility and Violations of the Law of One Price in Prediction Markets

## Abstract

Prediction markets are designed to aggregate dispersed information about future events, yet they operate on separate platforms without unified event definitions. This lack of standardization prevents liquidity pooling and creates persistent price discrepancies. The authors develop a semantic framework to identify economically identical events across platforms by analyzing natural-language descriptions, resolution semantics, and temporal scope.

## Key Findings

1. **Dataset Construction:** The authors created the first human-validated cross-platform dataset covering over 100,000 events across ten major venues from 2018-2025.

2. **Price Deviations:** Approximately 6% of events appear on multiple platforms, with semantically equivalent markets exhibiting persistent execution-aware price deviations of 2-4% on average.

3. **Structural Frictions:** The paper argues that prediction markets violate the Law of One Price for structural reasons that go beyond transaction costs, latency, or liquidity — semantic non-fungibility is a fundamental barrier to price convergence.

4. **Market Impact:** These persistent mispricings create arbitrage opportunities driven by structural frictions rather than informational disagreement.
