# Evidence on good forecasting practices from the Good Judgment Project

**Author:** Daniel Kokotajlo (with feedback from Katja Grace, Justis Mills, Tegan McCaslin, Carl Shulman, Jacob Lagerros)
**Published:** AI Impacts
**URL:** https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/

## Summary

Documents what the Good Judgment Project (GJP), led by Philip Tetlock and Barbara Mellers, identified as factors associated with successful forecasting. GJP won IARPA's 2011–2015 forecasting tournament by aggregating predictions from thousands of online volunteers on geopolitical questions.

## Top Factors for Forecasting Success (ranked by importance)

1. Prior performance in the same domain
2. Increased predictions on identical questions
3. Time spent deliberating
4. Team collaboration
5. Intelligence levels
6. Subject matter expertise
7. One-hour training modules
8. Cognitive reflection abilities
9. Active open-mindedness
10. Aggregation of multiple forecasts
11. Precise probabilistic thinking
12. Reference class forecasting (outside view)
13. Fermi estimation techniques
14. Bayesian reasoning approaches
15. Consistent practice

## The Superforecaster Profile

Successful forecasters are:
- Philosophically cautious and humble
- Intellectually curious
- Analytically minded
- Probabilistically skilled
- Demonstrating "active open-mindedness" (treating beliefs as testable hypotheses)
- Employing pragmatic methods: Fermi estimation, reference class forecasting, Bayesian updating
- Seeking diverse perspectives before finalizing probabilistic judgments

## Performance Results

- GJP consistently outperformed control groups by over 60%
- Surpassed intelligence community prediction markets populated by professional analysts with classified information access
- Teams were on average 23% more accurate than individuals

## Limitations

- Research focused primarily on near-term geopolitical events (typically within 2–5 years)
- Applicability to longer-term technological and societal forecasting is uncertain

## Relevance to Prediction-Market Pricing

Reference class forecasting and outside view are directly applicable to calibrating prior probabilities for Polymarket markets before examining current prices. The importance of domain-specific track records suggests specialization in specific market categories.
