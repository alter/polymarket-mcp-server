# A Note on Prediction Markets

## Abstract

The paper examines prediction markets where individuals sequentially place bets on future events. This process leaves a trail of personal probabilities for the event, each being conditional on the current individual's private background knowledge and information from prior announcements. The authors focus on two-person scenarios where participants start with identical overall distributions but possess different private information, taking turns updating their probabilities.

## Key Findings

- Announced probabilities converge toward a limiting value through iterative updates
- However, this limiting value may diverge from what would result if participants pooled their private information directly
- This observation highlights potential inefficiencies in sequential information revelation through prediction markets
- The mechanism's structure influences ultimate probability consensus beyond merely aggregating available knowledge
