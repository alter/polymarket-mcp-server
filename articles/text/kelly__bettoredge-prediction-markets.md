# Using Kelly Criterion with Prediction Markets

## Core Concept

The Kelly Criterion is a mathematical formula designed to determine optimal bet sizing in prediction markets. The formula is: **f* = (b × p – q) / b**, where f* represents the fraction of bankroll to wager, b is the odds offered, p is your probability estimate, and q equals 1 minus p.

## Key Applications

**Probability Assessment**: Success requires estimating true event probabilities and comparing them to market-implied odds. The difference between your assessment and the market's represents your "edge."

**Market Adaptation**: Prediction markets frequently misprice outcomes due to inefficiencies. The formula adjusts dynamically as odds shift with new information.

**Risk Management**: The method naturally suggests smaller bets when confidence is lower, providing built-in safeguards against overcommitting capital.

## Practical Implementation

A detailed example: For Federal Reserve rate hike predictions priced at $0.35, with a 50% probability estimate, the calculation yields approximately 23% of bankroll allocation.

Negative Kelly value indicates no recommended wager.

## Fractional Kelly Strategy

Rather than betting the full Kelly amount, many practitioners use "half-Kelly" or "quarter-Kelly" approaches. This conservative scaling reduces volatility while maintaining long-term growth potential, particularly valuable during uncertain market conditions.

## Success Principles

- Detailed record-keeping of all bets and estimates
- Resisting emotional adjustments to calculations
- Maintaining discipline across numerous bets
- Prioritizing value recognition over winner prediction
