# Prediction Markets? The Accuracy and Efficiency of $2.4 Billion in the 2024 Presidential Election

**Authors:** Joshua D. Clinton, TzuFeng Huang (Vanderbilt University)  
**Repository:** SocArXiv (d5yx2)  
**Coverage:** Iowa Electronic Markets, Kalshi, PredictIt, Polymarket  

## Dataset

- **2,500+ political prediction markets** from four platforms
- **$2.4 billion+ in transactions**
- Final 5 weeks of the 2024 U.S. presidential campaign
- Cross-platform price comparison for identical contracts

## Main Finding: Accuracy by Platform

| Platform | Markets outperforming chance |
|---------|------------------------------|
| PredictIt | 93% |
| Kalshi | 78% |
| Polymarket | 67% |

**Polymarket** — the largest by volume — was the **least accurate** of the four platforms.

## The Liquidity Paradox

More capital did not correlate with better predictions, controlling for event type.

**Why:** Massive liquidity attracted speculative "noise" and partisan "cheerleaders" who used markets to signal political identity rather than predict outcomes. PredictIt's $850 per-market cap (now $3,500 post-CFTC settlement) excluded whale positions, favored smaller deliberate participants.

## Efficiency Failures

Even the most accurate markets showed:
- Prices for identical contracts **diverged across exchanges**
- Daily price changes **weakly correlated or negatively autocorrelated**
- **Arbitrage opportunities peaked in the final two weeks** before Election Day
- Not all available information was synthesized across the ecosystem

## Niche Markets

Markets on "niche or low-information events" (winning margin, whether a candidate says a specific word) — least accurate. Speculation disguised as forecasting.

## Implications

Challenges three central claims made by prediction market advocates:
1. "More money → better predictions" — **false in this data**
2. "Prices efficiently aggregate information" — **weakly supported, violated by cross-platform divergence**
3. "Larger platforms are more reliable" — **reverse observed**

## Related Work

- Clinton & Huang studied alongside Tsang & Yang (arXiv:2603.03136) which found Polymarket matured in efficiency terms over the election cycle (but that work focused on a single market microstructure rather than cross-platform accuracy)
- CEPR analysis of 300,000+ Kalshi contracts found favorite-longshot bias but generally informative prices improving near resolution
