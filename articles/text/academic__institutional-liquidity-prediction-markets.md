# What Happens When Institutional Liquidity Enters Prediction Markets

## Abstract

The paper investigates how institutional liquidity affects prediction markets across several dimensions. It presents a research design for examining whether spreads narrow, price discovery strengthens, and whether all traders benefit equally when institutional capital enters. The paper uses a synthetic microstructure laboratory as a proof of concept for the measurement pipeline.

## Key Findings

1. **Differential mechanisms:** Market-maker coverage, liquidity incentives, and automation operate through distinct channels rather than unified pathways.

2. **Unequal distribution of gains:** Average liquidity improvements don't necessarily translate uniformly across all market participants.

3. **Vulnerability during stress:** Slower takers receive the least pass-through of tighter quoted markets during volatile periods, suggesting welfare losses concentrate among less-rapid traders.

4. **Key insight:** Once institutional liquidity enters prediction markets through maker programs, exchange-run incentives, and automated quoting, the market can look easier to trade on average while still becoming harsher for slower or less informed traders. A narrower quoted spread does not automatically mean that every participant actually receives better execution.
