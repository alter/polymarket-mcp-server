# Necessary and Sufficient Conditions for Frequency-Based Kelly Optimal Portfolio

**Author:** Chung-Han Hsieh  
**arXiv ID:** 2004.12099  
**Submitted:** April 25, 2020  
**Published:** IEEE Control Systems Letter, Vol. 5, No. 1, pp. 349-354, 2021

## Abstract Overview

The research addresses a discrete-time portfolio optimization problem involving multiple assets where trading frequency serves as an additional optimization parameter. The work uses the Kelly Criterion, which focuses on "maximizing the expected logarithmic growth of a trader's account."

## Key Contributions

The paper extends prior work by establishing necessary and sufficient conditions for frequency-based Kelly optimal portfolios. Major findings include:

- **Optimality Characterizations:** Introduces expected ratio optimality and asymptotic relative optimality measures
- **Extended Dominant Asset Theorem:** Proves that an asset dominates the portfolio if and only if the optimal portfolio contains exclusively that asset
- **Survivability Analysis:** Examines bankruptcy prevention within the trading framework
- **Practical Algorithm:** Proposes a trading algorithm using "dominant asset condition to decide when one should trigger a trade"

## Subject Areas

Mathematics, optimization, control systems, mathematical finance, portfolio management.

## Relevance

Addresses the question of how often to rebalance a Kelly-optimal portfolio, and whether high-frequency rebalancing always improves performance. Finds that in many scenarios, high-frequency rebalancing brings no additional benefit — the dominant asset should simply be held.
