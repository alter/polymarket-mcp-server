# Beyond the Polls: Quantifying Early Signals in Decentralized Prediction Markets with Cross-Correlation and Dynamic Time Warping

**Source:** MDPI Future Internet, Vol. 17, No. 11, Article 487  
**Published:** October 24, 2025

## Abstract

In response to the persistent failures of traditional election polling, this study introduces the **Decentralized Prediction Market Voter Framework (DPMVF)**, a novel tool to empirically test and quantify the predictive capabilities of Decentralized Prediction Markets (DPMs). The DPMVF is applied to Polymarket, analysing over **11 million on-chain transactions** from September 1 to November 5, 2024, benchmarked against aggregated polling in the 2024 U.S. Presidential Election across seven key swing states.

## Methodology

- **Cross-Correlation Function (CCF):** Linear analysis of lead-lag relationships
- **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW):** Non-linear pattern similarity analysis
- Non-parametric **Permutation Test** to validate observed alignments
- Coverage: 7 key swing states, Sept 1 – Nov 5, 2024
- 11+ million Polymarket on-chain transactions analyzed

## Key Findings

**Polymarket preceded polling shifts by up to 14 days** in contested states, confirmed as non-spurious with high confidence (p < 0.01) and exceptionally high correlation (up to 0.988).

**Divergent results across states:**
- **High-signal states (Arizona, Nevada, Pennsylvania):** Statistically significant early signals found — Polymarket led polling by up to 2 weeks
- **Low-signal states (North Carolina):** Weak signal environment — framework correctly identified low predictive value where polling was stable

## Framework Contribution

The DPMVF provides a **validated, descriptive tool** for contextualizing DPM signals. It moves beyond a simple "pass/fail" verdict on prediction market accuracy to provide a contextually-sensitive measure of when and where prediction markets add genuine informational value beyond polls.

## Significance

This paper provides empirical validation that decentralized prediction markets can meaningfully lead traditional polling data by up to two weeks in competitive electoral environments — but this lead relationship is state-conditional and not universal. The methodology generalizes to any decentralized prediction market with on-chain transaction data.
