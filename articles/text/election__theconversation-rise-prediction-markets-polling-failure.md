# How Polling Failures, Gambling Legalization and Political Gridlock Paved the Way for the Explosive Rise of Prediction Markets

**Source:** The Conversation  
**Date:** December 2025

## What Are Prediction Markets?

Prediction markets are betting platforms where users trade "event contracts" priced between 1–99 cents, paying $1 if an event occurs or nothing if it doesn't. Purchasing a contract represents a wager similar to sports betting, with traders able to buy and sell contracts as odds fluctuate.

## Theoretical Foundation

Two economic principles underpin prediction markets:
1. **Wisdom of Crowds:** The median judgment of a large, diverse group often surpasses individual expert predictions
2. **Efficient Market Hypothesis:** Prices reflect all available information through collective judgments of profit-seeking participants

## The Catalyst: Polling's Credibility Crisis

Traditional political polling faced mounting challenges:
- Declining response rates over decades
- Systematic undercounting of Trump voters in 2016 and 2020
- 2024 forecast: race was "a coin toss," while markets favored Trump at ~60% odds

## Historical Context

Political wagering has deep American roots. From the 1880s to 1930s, New York City was the hub of political wagering, sometimes exceeding the stock market in daily volume. The *New York Times* treated betting odds as reliable forecasting tools. The 1916 election saw ~$165 million (in 2002 dollars) wagered in organized New York markets.

## Legal Breakthrough (2024)

In October 2024, a federal court granted Kalshi legal authorization to operate prediction markets for U.S. elections. This triggered explosive growth:
- Over $500 million in presidential bets on Kalshi alone
- More than $3.6 billion in volume on Polymarket
- Billboard advertisements displaying live election odds in Times Square
- Major media partnerships: CNN, Wall Street Journal, CNBC

## Media Integration

News outlets now cite prediction markets as trusted sources with real-time advantages:
- Continuous updates vs. periodic polling snapshots
- Free public access to probability estimates
- Coverage beyond elections: Cabinet nominations, Supreme Court decisions

## The Insider Trading Problem

The article highlights two concerning cases:
- Anonymous accounts earned hundreds of thousands predicting Nicolás Maduro's downfall
- Suspicious timing preceded U.S. military actions

While Kalshi implemented enforcement against insider trading, regulatory oversight remains limited. The CFTC — much smaller than the SEC — oversees these contracts.

## Accuracy Questions

Research reveals potential biases: traders often "buy and sell only one position" rather than rationally switching between them, suggesting "wishful thinking bias." This mirrors sports bettors who wager "out of fandom or for entertainment."

## Cultural Significance

With Congress experiencing historic unproductivity and most Americans believing "they have little influence over the workings of the government," prediction markets offer ordinary citizens a sense of participation — transforming helpless observation into potential profit.
