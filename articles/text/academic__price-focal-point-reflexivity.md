# Price as Focal Point: Prediction Markets, Conditional Reflexivity, and the Politics of Common Knowledge

## Abstract

The paper examines prediction markets through a novel lens, arguing they function as coordination mechanisms beyond their traditional role as forecasting tools. Rather than solely asking whether markets predict accurately, the author investigates whether accurate forecasting is even the right criterion for a market that has become a public coordination device.

## Key Findings

- The research demonstrates that market signals' influence depends primarily on persistence, the breadth of responding trader types, and cross-platform consensus rather than market size alone
- The author introduces a Signal Credibility Index (SCI) combining three microstructure metrics to predict when price movements influence behavior
- Analysis of the 2024 U.S. presidential election reveals a systematic decoupling of social authority from epistemic robustness: the most visible market produced the least accurate forecasts
- Similar market shocks generated qualitatively distinct signal types with different implications for coordinating elite behavior
- The framework carries direct applications for regulating prediction markets as democratic information infrastructure
