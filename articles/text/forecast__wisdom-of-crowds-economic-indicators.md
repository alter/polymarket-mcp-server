# On the efficacy of the wisdom of crowds to forecast economic indicators

**Authors:** Nilton S. Siqueira Neto, José F. Fontanari
**Submitted:** July 18, 2022; revised September 24, 2022
**Published:** European Physical Journal B, 2023
**arXiv:** 2207.08924

## Abstract

Analyzed 15,455 forecasting contests using data from the Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia's Survey of Professional Forecasters to evaluate how well collective expert predictions perform.

## Key Findings

**Aggregation Method Comparison:**
- Median outperformed mean when combining expert forecasts
- Median: odds of 0.026 that crowd beats all participants (vs. 0.015 for mean)
- "Median is always guaranteed to beat the majority of participants, whereas the mean beats majority in only 67% of forecasts"
- Both methods yield ~20% average error vs. 31% for ARIMA time-series model

**Error Rates:**
- Both aggregation methods: ~20% average error
- Contest winners: 15% error
- ARIMA time-series model: 31% error
- Random individual forecasters: 22% error

**Key Conclusion:** "Selective attention is the most likely explanation for the mysterious high accuracy of the crowd reported in the literature" — challenging blanket assumptions about crowd wisdom effectiveness.

## Relevance to Prediction-Market Pricing

Establishes the 20% error floor for crowd aggregation on economic indicators. The median-over-mean finding directly informs aggregation strategies for combining probability forecasts. The ARIMA comparison shows crowd wisdom is robustly superior to pure time-series extrapolation.
