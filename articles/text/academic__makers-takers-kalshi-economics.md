# Makers and Takers: The Economics of the Kalshi Prediction Market

## Abstract

Using transaction-level data on over 300,000 contracts, this paper provides the first systematic evidence on Kalshi's pricing. The paper interprets patterns with a simple framework reflecting Kalshi's quote-driven microstructure, distinguishing Makers (well-informed traders who post offers) from Takers (who accept or reject these offers).

## Key Findings

- Kalshi's contract prices are informative and improve in accuracy as markets approach closing
- They display a clear favorite-longshot bias: low-price contracts win far less often than required to break even, while high-price contracts win more often and yield small positive returns
- Takers lose almost 32% on average, while makers have an average loss of about 10%
- There is a striking difference between the returns earned by makers and takers
- The favorite-longshot bias pattern is much stronger for takers than for makers, with far bigger losses for takers on their longshot contracts
