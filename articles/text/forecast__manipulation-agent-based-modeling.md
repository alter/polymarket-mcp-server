# Manipulation in Prediction Markets: An Agent-based Modeling Experiment

**Authors:** Bridget Smart, Ebba Mark, Anne Bastian, Josefina Waugh
**Submitted:** January 28, 2026
**arXiv:** 2601.20452

## Abstract

Investigates how well-funded actors ("whale" agents) can distort prediction market prices. Developed an agent-based simulation where traders possess varying expertise levels, private information, and budget constraints while observing public election forecasts. Biased whales can temporarily shift prices, with magnitude and duration of distortion increasing when non-whale bettors exhibit herding behavior and slow learning.

## Key Findings

- Markets exhibit price stability across broad parameter ranges through self-regulatory mechanisms
- High-budget minority agents can create temporary price distortions proportional to their capital share
- Distortion persistence depends on non-whale trader learning rates and herding intensity
- Herding behavior among ordinary traders amplifies and extends manipulative effects
- The $45M Polymarket bet (French national, 2024 election) temporarily pushed Trump odds — consistent with model predictions

## Methodology

- Agent-based simulation with heterogeneous traders
- Parameterized whale agents (budget fraction, bias magnitude, information quality)
- Non-whale trader models: informed, uninformed, herding
- Validated against empirical Polymarket data from 2024 election

## Conclusion

Classic assumption that individual traders are too small to affect prices no longer holds for large-budget actors. However, markets are self-correcting in most parameterizations — distortions are temporary. Herding behavior is the key amplifier.

## Relevance to Prediction-Market Pricing

Explains the whale-fade trading strategy: temporarily manipulated prices revert as informed traders arbitrage. The herding amplification mechanism suggests that price reversions are stronger in markets with more retail/uninformed participation.
