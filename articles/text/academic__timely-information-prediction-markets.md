# Timely Information from Prediction Markets

## Abstract

Prediction markets are powerful tools to elicit and aggregate beliefs from strategic agents. However, agents may exhaust social welfare by competing to be the first to update the market. This paper initiates the study of the trade-off between how quickly information is aggregated and how much this information costs.

## Key Findings

The authors examine two scenarios:
1. Where information becomes valueless after a deadline
2. Where the value of information decreases as time passes

They employ both theoretical analysis and computational simulations to demonstrate proposed mechanisms work effectively at incentivizing truthful, timely reporting while maximizing overall social welfare. The paper designs mechanisms that balance the speed of information aggregation against its costs, ensuring participants invest appropriate effort to gather data that is timely but not wastefully expensive.
